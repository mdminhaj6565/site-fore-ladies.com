The best women's golf shoe is one that grips the ground during your swing, stays comfortable for a five-mile walk, and suits the conditions you usually play in. Choose spikeless shoes for dry courses, carts and versatility; choose soft spikes for wet grass, hills and maximum stability. Then pick waterproof or breathable based on your climate, and fit the shoe to your foot late in the day.

Golf shoes are easy to overlook next to clothes, but they are the only equipment touching the ground on every shot. Here is how to choose.

Why golf shoes matter

Golf shoes give you traction as your weight shifts during the swing, so your feet stay planted while your body rotates. Ordinary running shoes are built to roll forward and can slip sideways on dewy grass or slopes.

They also protect the course. Golf shoe soles are designed not to tear up greens, which is why many courses don't allow metal spikes and some don't allow street shoes on the course at all.

Spiked vs spikeless: the main decision

Spikeless shoes have a molded rubber sole with many small lugs; spiked shoes have replaceable plastic cleats (often called soft spikes) screwed into the sole. Both are accepted almost everywhere.

A simple rule: if you mostly ride in a cart on dry, flat courses, spikeless is hard to beat. If you walk hilly courses or often play early in the morning when the grass is wet, soft spikes give you more confidence.

Waterproof or breathable?

Waterproof golf shoes use a membrane or waterproof fabric to keep dew and puddles out; breathable shoes use mesh or knit uppers that let heat escape. You usually trade one for the other.

  • Choose waterproof if you play early, in spring or autumn, or in a damp climate. Wet feet make a long round miserable.
  • Choose breathable if you play mostly in hot, dry weather. Mesh uppers feel cooler and lighter.
  • Many golfers own one of each and choose by the forecast.

How a golf shoe should fit

Golf shoes should feel snug through the heel and midfoot, with a little room for your toes. Your foot moves side to side during the swing, so a sloppy fit costs stability.

  • Shop late in the day. Feet swell after walking, and you will be walking for hours on the course.
  • Wear your golf socks when you try them on.
  • Check the heel. Your heel should not lift much as you walk or rise onto your toes.
  • Leave about a thumb's width of space in front of your longest toe.
  • Consider width. If shoes often feel tight across the ball of your foot, look for brands or models offered in wide sizes.
  • Compare to a shoe you love. Sizing varies between brands, especially European brands that use EU sizes.

Comfort for 18 holes

An 18-hole round on foot is a long walk, often several miles, so cushioning and support matter as much as grip. Look for a cushioned midsole, a supportive footbed (or room to use your own insole), and a lightweight upper.

Break new shoes in before an important round. Wear them on the range or for nine holes first, and keep blister plasters in your bag for the early outings.

Don't forget the socks

The right socks make a good shoe feel better. Choose moisture-wicking socks in a synthetic or merino blend, with some cushioning under the heel and ball of the foot. No-show socks suit skorts and dresses; ankle socks protect the back of the heel from rubbing in new shoes. Avoid cotton socks for long rounds, because damp cotton is the quickest route to blisters.

Caring for your golf shoes

Clean grass and mud off after each round with a soft brush, let shoes air-dry away from direct heat, and stuff wet shoes with newspaper to keep their shape. Check soft spikes regularly and replace them when they are worn smooth. Rotating two pairs lets each pair dry fully between rounds.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need golf shoes as a beginner?

Not on day one. Clean athletic shoes are usually fine at the driving range and for beginner lessons. Once you start playing full rounds, golf shoes are worth it for grip and comfort, and some courses require them. A spikeless pair is a versatile first choice because you can wear it to the range, the course and the café.

Are spikeless golf shoes as good as spiked?

On dry courses, spikeless shoes grip well and are more comfortable off the grass. On wet grass and steep slopes, soft spikes give more traction. Many golfers choose spikeless for everyday rounds and keep a spiked pair for wet seasons or hilly courses.

Can I wear sneakers to play golf?

At many public courses and driving ranges, clean sneakers are allowed. Some private clubs require golf shoes on the course. Sneakers can slip on wet grass because they aren't designed for sideways movement, so golf shoes give you a more stable swing, especially early in the morning.

Should golf shoes be tight or loose?

Snug, not tight. Your heel should be held firmly and your foot shouldn't slide side to side, but your toes need room to move. Leave about a thumb's width in front of your longest toe, and try shoes on late in the day with your golf socks.

Get the fit right first, then choose spiked or spikeless for your conditions, and you will forget you are wearing them — which is exactly the point.
