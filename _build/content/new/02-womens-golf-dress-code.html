A typical women's golf dress code asks for a top with a collar or mock neck, a skort, skirt, shorts or pants of a "reasonable" length, no denim, and golf shoes. Sleeveless tops are usually allowed if they have a collar. The details change from course to course, because each club writes its own rules.

That last point causes most of the confusion. There is no single official golf dress code for women, so the same outfit can be perfect at a municipal course and questioned at a private club twenty minutes away. Here is how to read the rules, what the common sticking points are, and how to pack an outfit that works almost anywhere.

Who sets golf dress codes?

Each course or club sets its own dress code. The Rules of Golf cover how the game is played, not what players wear, so attire rules come from the facility you are visiting.

You will usually find the dress code on the course website under "course policies", "etiquette" or "plan your visit". Resorts often put it on the booking confirmation. If you can't find it, the pro shop staff answer this question every day and won't mind you asking.

The parts of a women's dress code that vary most

Five items cause most of the questions: collars, sleeveless tops, length of skorts and shorts, leggings, and shoes. Everything else — prints, colors, brands — is almost never regulated.

Collars and necklines

A collar is the classic requirement, and a mock neck is accepted at nearly every course that asks for one. Many courses have relaxed their rules for women's tops, so crew necks and stylish collarless golf tops are often fine at public and semi-private courses. Private clubs are the most likely to insist on a collar or mock neck.

Sleeveless tops

Sleeveless golf shirts are a staple of women's golf and are widely accepted when they have a collar or mock neck. Racerback tanks, spaghetti straps and tops that show a sports bra are the items most often listed as not allowed.

Skort, skirt and shorts length

Length rules are usually written vaguely — "appropriate length" or "mid-thigh" — and a few clubs give a measurement. For reference, when the LPGA tightened its guidelines for tour players in July 2017, the rule was that skirts, skorts and shorts had to be long enough to cover the bottom when standing or bending over, according to AP reporting at the time. That rule applies to professional events, not your Saturday round, but many clubs use similar language. Our skort length guide explains how lengths are measured.

Leggings and joggers

Leggings are where dress codes disagree most. Many public courses accept golf leggings, especially in cold weather, while stricter clubs often don't allow them worn on their own. Leggings under a skort or dress are accepted far more widely. The 2017 LPGA guidelines for tour players also restricted leggings, which is part of why some private clubs still list them.

Shoes

Golf shoes with soft spikes or spikeless soles are accepted everywhere. Many courses no longer allow metal spikes because they damage greens, and some stricter clubs don't allow street sneakers on the course. A clean pair of athletic shoes is usually fine at the range.

The clubhouse has its own rules

Many clubs apply a separate dress code in the clubhouse, dining room or bar. Common rules include taking hats off indoors, changing out of golf shoes, and smart-casual dress for evening dining. If you are staying for lunch, check whether your on-course outfit works inside, or pack a light layer to throw on.

An outfit that passes almost every dress code

If you don't know the course, choose the most widely accepted version of each piece. It is not the only option, just the one least likely to cause a problem.

  • A collared or mock-neck golf top, sleeveless or with sleeves — see women's golf tops.
  • A skort that sits at mid-thigh or longer, tailored golf shorts, or golf pants — see golf skorts and golf pants.
  • Or a golf dress with a collar and built-in shorts.
  • Golf shoes with soft spikes or spikeless soles.
  • A belt if your bottoms have belt loops, plus a visor or cap for sun.

For cooler days, add a quarter-zip, vest or wind jacket from golf outerwear. For more ideas, read what to wear golfing.

Frequently asked questions

Are sleeveless golf shirts allowed for women?

At most courses, yes, as long as the top has a collar or mock neck. Sleeveless collared golf shirts are standard women's golf wear. Tops with spaghetti straps, racerbacks or deep armholes that show a sports bra are the styles most likely to be turned away at stricter private clubs.

Is there a minimum skort length for golf?

Some clubs set one, many just say "appropriate length", and public courses rarely measure anything. When a club does give guidance, it is usually around mid-thigh. If you are unsure, a skort of about 16 to 18 inches from the waistband suits most heights and most rules; taller women may prefer longer.

Can women wear leggings on a golf course?

It depends on the course. Many public courses allow golf leggings, especially in cold weather, but stricter clubs often don't allow them on their own. Leggings worn under a skort or golf dress are accepted much more widely. Ask the pro shop before you go if you plan to wear them alone.

Can I wear a tennis skirt to play golf?

Often yes, if it is long enough for the course and paired with a collared top. Tennis skirts tend to be shorter than golf skorts and usually lack pockets, which matters when you need somewhere for a ball and tees. Our guide on wearing tennis clothes for golf covers the differences.

What happens if my outfit breaks the dress code?

Usually a polite word from the pro shop and a suggestion to change, sometimes with an offer to buy something in the shop. It is rarely dramatic. Checking the dress code ahead of time, especially for private clubs, resorts and tournaments, saves you the awkward conversation.

Dress codes look strict on paper, but they mostly come down to looking neat and wearing proper golf shoes. Check the course, pick the safe version of each piece if you are unsure, and spend your energy on the round instead.
