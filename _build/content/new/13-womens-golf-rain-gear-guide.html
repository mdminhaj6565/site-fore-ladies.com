To golf in the rain, you need a waterproof jacket that lets you swing freely, waterproof shoes, a pair of rain gloves, and an umbrella and towels to keep your hands and grips dry. Rain pants or a quick-drying skort with leggings handle the lower half. With the right gear, a wet day on the course is far more pleasant than it sounds — and often much quieter.

Rain gear is the part of a golf wardrobe most people buy last and wish they had bought first. Here is what matters and what to look for.

Waterproof vs water-resistant

Waterproof gear keeps rain out for a long time, usually thanks to a membrane or coated waterproof fabric and sealed seams. Water-resistant gear sheds light showers and drizzle but soaks through in steady rain.

  • Water-resistant wind jackets are light and breathable, ideal for misty mornings and passing showers.
  • Waterproof jackets are what you want when rain is forecast for most of the round.
  • Breathability matters on both. Walking 18 holes generates heat, and a jacket that doesn't breathe leaves you damp from the inside.

Choosing a golf rain jacket

A golf rain jacket should be quiet, light and cut so it doesn't restrict your swing. General-purpose raincoats are often too long, too stiff or too noisy for golf.

What to check:

  • Freedom of movement: make a full practice swing in it. The shoulders shouldn't pull and the sleeves shouldn't ride up past your wrists.
  • Length: it should cover your waistband without bunching when you bend at address.
  • Quiet fabric: crinkly fabric is distracting at the top of the backswing.
  • Adjustable cuffs and hem to keep water out.
  • Packability: a jacket that folds into a pocket of your golf bag gets carried; a bulky one stays in the car.

Short-sleeve and sleeveless rain tops exist too, and some golfers prefer them because nothing touches the forearms during the swing. See golf jackets and all golf outerwear.

Keeping your lower half dry

Rain pants are the most complete solution: pull them on over your golf pants or leggings and they keep you dry to the shoe. Look for side zips at the ankle so you can get them on and off without removing your shoes.

If you prefer a skort, a quick-drying skort over thermal or water-resistant leggings works well in light rain. Avoid cotton anywhere in your outfit on a wet day; it gets heavy and cold.

Hands, grips and gloves

Wet hands are the biggest threat to your game in the rain, because a slipping grip ruins any swing. Golf rain gloves, sold as pairs, are designed to grip better as they get wet, which is the opposite of a regular leather glove.

  • Keep regular gloves in a zip pocket and swap to rain gloves when it starts to pour.
  • Bring two towels: one to keep dry under the umbrella or inside the bag hood, one to wipe clubs and balls.
  • Dry each grip before you swing.
  • A rain hood for your golf bag keeps grips and towels dry between shots.

Shoes, hat and umbrella

Shoes: waterproof golf shoes are essential for wet rounds. Soft-spiked soles give better grip than spikeless soles on wet grass and slopes. Bring a spare pair of socks. Our guide to choosing women's golf shoes covers the options.

Hat: a cap or bucket hat keeps rain off your face and glasses; a visor won't protect the top of your head.

Umbrella: a large golf umbrella shelters you and your bag between shots. Hook a towel inside the spokes to keep it dry. Never use an umbrella during a thunderstorm — leave the course when there is lightning.

Caring for rain gear

Let wet gear dry fully at room temperature before packing it away, never folded up in the bag. Wash waterproof jackets only as often as the label suggests, with a detergent suitable for technical outerwear, and skip fabric softener, which can make waterproof and breathable fabrics perform worse. Frequently asked questions

What should I wear golfing in the rain?

Wear a waterproof golf jacket over a light moisture-wicking layer, rain pants or golf pants (or a quick-dry skort with leggings), waterproof golf shoes, a cap and rain gloves. Bring two towels and an umbrella. Avoid cotton, which holds water and gets heavy and cold.

Are rain gloves worth it for golf?

Yes, if you play in wet weather often. Rain gloves are designed to grip better when they are wet, which keeps the club from slipping. They are usually sold as a pair. Keep a dry regular glove in your bag for when the rain stops.

What is the difference between a golf rain jacket and a wind jacket?

A rain jacket is waterproof, usually with sealed seams, and keeps steady rain out. A wind jacket blocks wind and handles light drizzle but soaks through in real rain. Wind jackets are lighter and more breathable; many golfers carry one for cool days and a rain jacket when rain is forecast.

Should I play golf in the rain?

Light to moderate rain is fine with the right gear, and courses are often quieter. Stop immediately if there is thunder or lightning; open golf courses are dangerous in storms. Some courses close or restrict carts after heavy rain to protect the turf, so check with the course.
