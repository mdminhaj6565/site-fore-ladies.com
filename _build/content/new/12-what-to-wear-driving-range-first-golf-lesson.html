For your first golf lesson or a trip to the driving range, wear comfortable athletic clothes that let you rotate freely — a fitted T-shirt or polo, stretchy pants, shorts or a skort — and clean athletic shoes or golf shoes. Most public ranges have little or no dress code. If the lesson is at a private club, wear a collared or mock-neck top and golf bottoms to be safe.

Starting golf can feel like there are a lot of unwritten rules. What you wear to the range isn't one of the hard ones. Here is how to dress so you can focus on the lesson.

Comfort and movement come first

The golf swing is a full-body rotation, so choose clothes that stretch and don't restrict your shoulders, hips or knees. If you can do a deep squat and swing your arms over your head without anything pulling, you are dressed for a lesson.

Avoid very loose sleeves or oversized hoodies on top. Your instructor needs to see your posture and arm position, and baggy clothing can catch on the club or hide what you are doing.

What to wear on top

A fitted athletic T-shirt, a polo or a mock-neck top all work. Choose a moisture-wicking fabric if you can, because hitting a bucket of balls warms you up quickly.

  • Public range or simulator: a neat athletic tee or tank is usually fine.
  • Lesson at a club: a collared polo or mock-neck top is the safe choice. See women's golf tops.
  • Layers: outdoor ranges are often breezy. A light quarter-zip or vest you can take off between shots is ideal.

What to wear on the bottom

Stretchy pants, athletic shorts, golf shorts or a skort are all good for a lesson. The key is freedom to bend your knees and tilt forward from the hips without anything digging in.

  • Leggings or yoga pants are comfortable for lessons and usually fine at public ranges, though some private clubs don't allow them on their own.
  • A golf skort gives you the "golf look" with built-in shorts, so bending to tee up a ball is easy. See golf skorts.
  • Avoid jeans: denim restricts the swing and isn't allowed at many golf facilities.

Shoes for the range

Clean athletic shoes with flat, grippy soles are fine for most ranges and lessons, especially on mats. Running shoes with very soft, thick heels can feel unstable during a swing; a flatter trainer is better.

If your lesson is on grass, especially early in the morning, golf shoes help your feet stay planted. A spikeless pair is a good first purchase because you can wear it at the range, on the course and around town. Our guide to choosing women's golf shoes explains the options.

A few range habits worth learning early

Range etiquette matters more than your outfit, and it is easy to learn. These habits will make you feel at home on your first visit.

  • Stay behind the line. Hit only from the marked tee area or your mat, and never walk forward onto the range to collect balls.
  • Give neighbors space. Keep a club's length or more between you and the next golfer, and wait until they finish their swing before walking behind them.
  • Keep your phone quiet and conversations low, especially during lessons nearby.
  • Leave the bay tidy. Return empty baskets, pick up your tees and replace divots if you hit off grass.

Nobody expects a beginner to know everything. If you are unsure, ask the range staff or your instructor — they are used to helping new golfers.

What to bring to your first lesson

  • A golf glove: worn on your lead hand (the left hand for right-handed golfers). It helps grip and prevents blisters. Ask your instructor if you are unsure which size.
  • Water and a small snack.
  • Sun protection: a cap or visor and sunscreen for outdoor ranges.
  • Clubs, if you have them. Many instructors and ranges provide clubs for beginners; ask when you book.
  • A hair tie if you have long hair — it keeps your view of the ball clear in the wind.

You don't need a full golf wardrobe to start. Add pieces as you play more, beginning with a skort or golf shorts, a couple of collared tops and a pair of golf shoes. When you are ready for your first round, read what to wear golfing and our dress code guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear leggings to a golf lesson?

Yes, at most public ranges and lesson facilities leggings are fine and very comfortable for learning the swing. Some private clubs don't allow leggings on their own, so if your lesson is at a club, wear golf pants or a skort, or wear leggings under a skort.

Do I need golf shoes for a lesson?

Not for your first lessons. Clean athletic shoes with a flat, grippy sole work on range mats and most lesson tees. If you take lessons on grass, especially when it is damp, golf shoes help with grip. Many beginners buy golf shoes once they start playing full rounds.

What should I wear to Topgolf or an indoor simulator?

Comfortable casual or athletic clothes are usually fine at golf entertainment venues and simulators. Choose clothes that let you swing, and check whether the venue has rules about shoes. A golf glove helps if you plan to hit a lot of balls.

Is a golf glove necessary for beginners?

It isn't required, but most golfers find a glove helps them hold the club consistently and prevents blisters while learning. Right-handed golfers wear it on the left hand. Your instructor can help you check the fit, which should be snug with no loose material at the fingertips.

Dress comfortably, bring a glove and some water, and let your instructor worry about the swing — the outfit is the easy part.
