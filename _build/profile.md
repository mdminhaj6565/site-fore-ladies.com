# Fore Ladies Golf — brand voice profile

Use this for every page and article. Content is in US English.

## Who we are
- Fore Ladies Golf: a woman-owned, woman-operated women's golf apparel business, founded in 2017 by a golfer (Jessica) in Plainfield, Illinois, who couldn't find golf clothes she liked in big-box stores.
- Catalog: women's golf clothes (tops, skorts, pants, shorts, golf dresses, outerwear), tennis apparel, golf shoes, bags, accessories, gift and subscription boxes (the "Tee Box").
- Current status: **online checkout is not available on this website.** Product pages are a style and sizing reference. Never write "buy now", "add to cart", "in stock", "free shipping", discount codes or anything that implies an order can be placed.

## Voice
- Warm, confident, practical — a golfer talking to another golfer. Light humor is fine (the brand's line: "women's golf clothing doesn't have to be a hazard").
- "You" voice. Short and medium sentences mixed. Plain words over jargon; explain a term once when it's needed (outseam, UPF, base layer).
- Start every H2 with a direct answer in the first one or two sentences, then the detail.
- Inclusive of every age, size and skill level. Never body-shame or tell readers what they "can't" wear; talk about fit, comfort and dress codes instead.
- Sign-off and byline: "the Fore Ladies Golf team". Never invent a named writer, pro, customer or quote.

## Facts and claims
- No invented statistics, studies, prices, reviews, testimonials or personal anecdotes ("I tested…", "our customers say…").
- When a fact needs a source, link a real one (Skin Cancer Foundation, USGA, LPGA, brand care labels) and say what it means for the reader.
- Prices only as "last listed price" from product data. Never promise availability.
- Dress codes vary by course: always tell readers to check their own club or course.

## Avoid
- "delve", "in conclusion", "whether you're a … or a …", "elevate your game", "game-changer", "look no further", "unlock", stacked adjectives.
- Keyword stuffing, fake urgency, emojis in body copy.
- Copying text from brand sites, lyrics, quotes from books or films, images of known characters.
