How to Make a DIY Golf Gift Box for a Woman Golfer

By Fore Ladies Golf team · November 13, 2026

To make a DIY golf gift box for a woman golfer, pick one theme that fits how she plays, choose one "anchor" gift, add two or three small things she uses up every round, finish with one personal touch, and pack it all in a box or bag she can keep using. That formula keeps every item useful.

What goes in a DIY golf gift box?

A good golf gift box has four layers: one anchor gift, a few useful consumables, one comfort or fun item, and a personal note.

  • The anchor. The one thing she'll notice first: a layer, a headcover, a towel in a print she loves, or a lesson voucher. Spend most of your budget here.
  • The consumables. Golfers always run out of balls, tees, gloves and sunscreen. A sleeve of balls, a pack of durable tees from our golf tees section or a fresh glove gets used within a week.
  • The comfort or fun item. Hand warmers, a snack she likes for the turn, a card game for the group, or a cozy throw for the drive home.
  • The personal touch. A handwritten card, a ball marker in her favorite colors, or an invitation to play a round together.

If you only remember one rule, make it this: every item should be something she'll reach for on the course or right after it. Our golf stocking stuffers guide has plenty of small fillers that fit the consumables layer.

Pick a theme before you shop

A theme makes the box feel planned and makes shopping faster. Start with the season she plays in and the kind of golfer she is.

The winter golfer. If she plays through the cold months, build around warmth: a sweater, pullover or vest from our golf outerwear range as the anchor, plus a few packs of hand warmers and a winter-weight glove if she uses one.

The sunny-day golfer. For warm-weather rounds or a golf trip south, think sun care. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, reapplied about every two hours. A sunscreen stick, an SPF lip balm and a visor or hat make a tidy set. Check the expiry date on anything you buy for a box.

The beginner. For someone new to the game, keep it simple and encouraging: balls she won't mind losing, tees, a towel and a voucher for a lesson or range session. Our guide to gifts for women new to golf explains what beginners actually need first.

The after-the-round box. Some golfers love the social side as much as the golf. A cozy throw, a fun golf tee shirt and a card game for her group fit this one well. Our throw blankets section had plush options like the Vera Bradley Blue Penguin throw (last listed price $55.00).

Choose a box she'll keep using

The best container is part of the gift. Choose something she can use again, and size it so the contents fit snugly with a little room for padding.

A sturdy shoebox or a plain craft box with a lid is the classic choice and easy to decorate. A reusable tote, a small cooler bag or a golf shoe bag makes a smarter container, because she'll carry it long after the gift is opened. For a very small set, like a marker, a few tees and a repair tool, a zip pouch or a driver headcover can hold everything; our guide to choosing golf headcovers explains which sizes fit which clubs.

Avoid boxes much bigger than the contents; empty space lets things slide around and makes the box look half-full.

How to handle sizes without spoiling the surprise

If the anchor is clothing or a glove, find her size before you buy, or choose a size-free anchor instead.

Check labels quietly: glove sizes are usually printed inside the wrist strap, and tops and skorts have a size tag at the back or waistband. Sizes vary from brand to brand, so compare what you find against our women's golf size charts. If you can't find out, go size-free. Headcovers, towels, throws, markers and vouchers all work without guessing, and you can still add something to wear later once you know her size.

How to pack it so it looks good and nothing rattles

Pack the heaviest, flattest item at the bottom, fill the gaps with soft items, and put the card on top so it's the first thing she sees.

  1. Line the box. Tissue paper or a folded golf towel makes a neat base and doubles as part of the gift.
  2. Anchor first. Lay folded clothing or the throw flat at the bottom. Stand a headcover upright at one end.
  3. Group the small things. Golf balls usually come in sleeves of three, and CaddieHQ notes a standard dozen is packed as four sleeves of three. A single sleeve sits neatly on its side. Put loose tees and markers in a small pouch so they don't scatter.
  4. Fill the gaps. Crinkle paper or more tissue stops items from sliding.
  5. Card on top, then close. Tie the lid with ribbon or twine.

If you want the wrapping to be recyclable, the Suffolk recycling guide suggests the scrunch test: if paper scrunches into a ball and stays scrunched, it can usually be recycled, while glitter and foil types can't. Local rules differ, so check yours.

Write a card that makes it personal

The card is what turns a box of golf supplies into a gift. Keep it short and specific to her game.

Mention something real: the course you played together, the putt she finally holed, or the season she's looking forward to. A promise works well too, like an invitation to a round, a range session or a lesson you'll book together. Write it in your own words.

When a ready-made box makes more sense

A DIY box is best when you know her taste well. If you don't, or you're short on time, a curated box is the easier route.

Frequently asked questions

What should I put in a golf gift box for a woman?

Start with one anchor gift, such as a layer, a headcover or a towel she'll love. Add two or three things she uses up, like a sleeve of balls, durable tees, a glove or an SPF 30+ sunscreen stick. Finish with one fun or cozy extra and a handwritten card. Keep every item useful on or after the course.

What can I use as the box for a DIY golf gift?

A sturdy shoebox or craft box with a lid works well and is easy to decorate. For a gift that keeps giving, use something she can reuse: a tote, a small cooler bag or a golf shoe bag. For a few small items, a zip pouch or a headcover can hold everything neatly.

How do I make a golf gift box without knowing her size?

Choose a size-free anchor. Headcovers, towels, throw blankets, ball markers, tees and lesson vouchers all work without a size. If you'd like to include clothing, check the tags on what she already wears and compare them with a size chart, because sizes vary between brands. Otherwise, add a clothing gift later.

How many golf balls should I put in a gift box?

One sleeve is enough for most boxes. Golf balls usually come in sleeves of three, and a standard dozen is four sleeves. A single sleeve fits neatly beside other items and leaves room for the rest of the gift. If she plays often or loses a few balls each round, a full dozen makes a generous anchor.
