Women's Golf Gloves: How to Find Your Size and When to Replace Them

By Fore Ladies Golf team · November 15, 2026

To find your women's golf glove size, measure your lead hand (the left hand for a right-handed golfer) in two places: the length of your middle finger and the width around your palm. Match both numbers on the brand's size chart, try the glove on, and look for a snug, second-skin fit with no loose material at the fingertips. Replace it when the palm or thumb turns shiny, thin or torn, or when it stops feeling tacky.

Which hand do you wear a golf glove on?

Most golfers wear one glove on their lead hand: the left hand if you swing right-handed, the right hand if you swing left-handed. As Golf Monthly explains, with a conventional grip the lead hand sits at the top of the club and guides it through the swing, so that's the hand that needs the extra grip and protection.

This matters when you shop, because gloves are sold by the hand they fit, not the hand you write with. A right-handed golfer looks for a glove "for the left hand," sometimes labeled "LH" or "worn on left." Some golfers wear a glove on both hands, and that's common with rain and winter gloves, which are often sold as pairs.

How to measure your hand for a golf glove

You need two measurements, and a soft tape measure makes it easy. FootJoy's women's glove fitting guide describes them like this:

  1. Finger length. Hold your lead hand out flat and measure your middle finger from the top edge of your palm (where the finger meets the hand) to the tip of the finger.
  2. Palm width. Wrap the tape around your hand at the first knuckles and across the palm, leaving your thumb out.

Write both numbers down, then find where they meet on the brand's chart. Sizes aren't identical from brand to brand, so always use the chart for the glove you're looking at rather than one general table.

FootJoy adds three useful tips. If your finger length number is smaller than your palm width number, try a "cadet" glove, a cut made for shorter fingers and a wider palm. If you fall between two sizes, try the smaller one first. And check your size at the start of every season, because it can change over time.

Measuring is worth the two minutes. In a GOLF.com article on glove lifespan, FootJoy reported that upward of 65 percent of golfers use a glove that is too large.

How should a golf glove fit?

A golf glove should fit like a second skin: snug across the palm and the back of the hand, with the fingertips filling the ends of the fingers and no loose material. It should never feel so tight that it cuts off circulation or limits how you close your hand.

Try this check when you put one on:

  • Fingertips. Your fingers reach the ends. Extra fabric at the tips means the glove is too long.
  • Palm. Make a fist around a club grip. The palm shouldn't bunch or wrinkle. Wrinkles are where a glove slips and wears first.
  • Closure. The tab closes comfortably without straining to reach. If you have to pull it hard, the glove is too small. If it overlaps far past the patch, it's too big.
  • Cuff. Pull the glove on by the fingers, not the cuff. Golf Monthly warns against adjusting a glove by tugging the cuff, since it stretches the glove out of shape.

Always try a glove on before you settle on a size, and if you're unsure, compare two neighboring sizes side by side with a club in your hand.

Leather vs synthetic vs rain gloves compared

Leather gives the best feel, synthetic lasts longer (GOLF.com notes a delicate cabretta glove isn't apt to last as long as a thick synthetic one), and rain or winter gloves handle bad weather. If you play year-round, it can make sense to keep more than one type in your bag.

When should you replace your golf glove?

Replace your glove when it stops gripping or starts to wear through, not after a set number of rounds. There's no official lifespan. GOLF.com points out that online estimates range from two rounds to 20, and FootJoy says there are too many factors to give a hard figure. Golf Monthly puts a leather glove at roughly 15 to 20 rounds. A winter of indoor practice counts too: our golf simulator guide explains what to bring for a session of back-to-back swings.

Look for these signs:

  • Shiny, smooth patches on the palm or the heel of the hand, where the leather has lost its tack.
  • Thin spots or holes, usually at the thumb pad, palm or fingertips.
  • Stiff or crusty leather after it got soaked and dried badly.
  • Stretched-out fit: the glove now wrinkles in the palm or slides when you swing.

Where the glove wears can also tell you something about your grip. Golf Monthly notes that heavy thumb wear can mean your thumb sits too far down the grip, and palm wear can mean the club is slipping or sitting too much in your palm.

How to make a golf glove last longer

Take it off between shots, let it dry flat at room temperature, and rotate two or three gloves. Those three habits do most of the work.

  • Dry it gently. Pat a damp glove dry and let it air-dry. Golf Monthly warns that a hairdryer or radiator will turn leather hard and crusty, and it may crack.
  • Store it flat. GOLF.com suggests keeping gloves in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, ideally smoothed out and back in their original packaging. A glove left balled up in a hot car or bag pocket loses its shape fast.
  • Rotate. Swapping gloves during a round and between practice and play spreads the wear. On hot or wet days, a spare in a zip pocket helps a lot.
  • Clean it lightly. GOLF.com mentions regular cleaning with mild soap and water. Check the care label first, since leather and synthetic gloves can differ.

Frequently asked questions

What size golf glove does the average woman wear?

There's no single average size worth relying on, because hands vary so much and every brand cuts its gloves a little differently. Measure your finger length and palm width, use the chart for the exact glove you want, and try it on. If you're between sizes, start with the smaller one.

What is a cadet golf glove?

A cadet glove is cut for a hand with shorter fingers relative to a wider palm. FootJoy suggests trying a cadet size when your finger length measurement is smaller than your palm width measurement. If regular gloves fit your palm but leave empty fabric at the fingertips, a cadet size is worth a try.

Should a golf glove be tight or loose?

Snug, but not tight. It should sit close across the palm and back of the hand, with your fingertips reaching the ends and no wrinkles when you grip a club. A loose glove slides, rubs and wears out faster. A glove that's too tight restricts your hand and can feel like it's cutting off circulation.

Do I need a golf glove as a beginner?

No rule says you must wear one, and some golfers never do. Many beginners like a glove because it helps them hold the club without squeezing and can reduce blisters during practice. A durable synthetic or hybrid glove is a sensible first pick, since new golfers often hit a lot of range balls.

A glove is one of the smallest things in your bag and one of the easiest to get right.
