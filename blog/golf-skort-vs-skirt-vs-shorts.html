Golf Skort vs Golf Skirt vs Golf Shorts: Which Should You Wear?

By Fore Ladies Golf team · October 25, 2026

A golf skort is a skirt with built-in shorts, a golf skirt has no shorts (or only a light liner), and golf shorts are tailored shorts cut for the swing. For most women the skort is the easiest everyday choice because it gives skirt style with shorts-level coverage. Shorts win on hot, windy or hilly days; a skirt suits players who want the lightest feel and don't mind wearing their own under-shorts.

The right answer depends on how you play, the weather and the course. This comparison covers what actually changes from one to the other.

What is the difference between a skort and a golf skirt?

The difference is the inner layer. A skort has shorts sewn into it, usually a stretchy pair that sits close to the leg. A golf skirt is a skirt alone, sometimes with a thin liner, so most players wear their own shorts or briefs underneath.

Because the terms are used loosely, some brands call a skort a "golf skirt". The product details settle it: look for "built-in shorts", "inner short" or "attached liner".

When a skort is the best choice

Choose a skort if you want a skirt look without thinking about coverage. Bending to tee up, retrieve a ball from the cup or crouch to read a putt is a big part of every round, and built-in shorts make all of it a non-issue.

Skorts also tend to have practical extras: ball pockets in the inner shorts, wide waistbands that keep a polo tucked in, and stretch fabrics that move with your rotation. If you are building a golf wardrobe from scratch, two or three skorts in neutral colors go with almost every top. Our skort length guide helps you pick the right length, and you can browse golf skorts.

When golf shorts are better

Golf shorts are best on very hot, windy or hilly courses, and for anyone who simply prefers shorts. With one layer of fabric, they are cooler at the top of the leg than a skort, and there is nothing to blow around on an exposed links-style course.

Look for a tailored cut with belt loops, a flat front and a modest inseam. Many golfers find inseams around five to nine inches the most comfortable, but check your course: very short shorts are the ones most likely to break a dress code. Stretch fabric matters here too, because woven shorts without stretch can pull across the hips when you swing. See golf shorts.

When a golf skirt makes sense

A golf skirt is the lightest option and often has the most movement and flow, which some players love for style. It suits golfers who already have favorite under-shorts and want the freedom to pair them.

The trade-offs are fewer pockets and more attention needed on windy days and when bending. If you like the look of a skirt but want the convenience of a skort, check whether the brand offers the same style both ways.

Fabric and fit details that matter for all three

Whatever you choose, the fabric decides how it feels at hole 15. Performance blends of polyester or nylon with spandex dry quickly, resist wrinkles and stretch with your swing.

  • Stretch: do a practice swing and a deep squat in the fitting room or at home. Anything that pulls across the hips will bother you on the course.
  • Waist: a wide, flat waistband or a shaped waist with belt loops keeps tops tucked and stops the waistband rolling.
  • Inner shorts: for skorts, check that the shorts are long enough not to ride up and are made of a smooth, breathable fabric.
  • Color: dark and bright colors hide grass marks better than white or pastels.

Our take: build a small rotation

Most women end up owning a mix: a couple of skorts for everyday rounds, one pair of tailored golf shorts for hot or windy days, and a golf dress or skirt for when they want something different. That way the weather and the course decide the outfit, not the one clean bottom in the drawer. If you like a one-piece option, read our golf dress guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is a skort better than shorts for golf?

Neither is better for your game; it is a comfort and style choice. Skorts give a skirt look with built-in coverage, while shorts are cooler and more practical in wind. Many golfers keep both and choose by the weather. Both are accepted at most courses at a reasonable length.

Do golf skorts have pockets?

Most do, though the location varies. Some skorts have side pockets in the skirt layer, while others hide pockets in the inner shorts, which keeps a ball from swinging around as you walk. If pockets matter to you, check the product details before choosing, because a few fashion-led styles have none.

What do you wear under a golf skirt?

Most players wear fitted under-shorts, bike-style shorts or sport briefs under a golf skirt that has no built-in shorts. Choose a smooth, breathable fabric in a color that matches the skirt, and make sure the shorts are slightly shorter than the skirt so they don't show when you walk.

Are golf skorts allowed at private clubs?

Yes, skorts are standard golf attire and accepted at private clubs as long as they meet the club's length rule. Many clubs ask for around mid-thigh. If you are visiting a strict club, a skort of 18 inches or longer is a safe choice. Our dress code guide explains the other common rules.

Pick the one that matches the day — skort for everyday, shorts for heat and wind, skirt for flow — and you will always have the right bottom for the round ahead.
