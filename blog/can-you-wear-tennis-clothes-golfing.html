Can You Wear Tennis Clothes Golfing? Tennis vs Golf Apparel Explained

By Fore Ladies Golf team · October 25, 2026

You can often wear tennis clothes for golf, but not every piece crosses over. A tennis skort or skirt usually works if it is long enough for the course and paired with a collared or mock-neck top. Collarless tennis tanks and very short tennis skirts are the items most likely to break a golf dress code. Tennis dresses without a collar are fine at relaxed courses but risky at private clubs.

Plenty of women play both sports, and it makes sense to want clothes that do double duty. Here is how tennis and golf apparel differ and how to mix them well.

How tennis and golf clothes differ

The two sports ask different things of clothing. Tennis is short bursts of running, so tennis clothes are cut short and light for speed. Golf means hours of walking and bending, with dress codes that lean traditional, so golf clothes are cut longer and more often have collars and pockets.

Typical tennis apparelTypical golf apparel
Skirt or skort lengthOften about 13–14 inchesOften about 15–20 inches
NecklinesTanks, racerbacks, crew necksCollars, mock necks, collared sleeveless tops
PocketsBall pockets in the shorts, sometimes nonePockets for balls, tees and markers
FabricVery light, maximum airflowLight to mid-weight, often UPF-rated
Dress expectationsRelaxed at most clubs; some require whitesVaries by course; private clubs strictest

In our own catalog, for example, the Lucky In Love tennis skirts are listed at 13.5 and 14 inches, while golf skorts from brands such as Daily Sports come in 15- to 20-inch lengths.

Tennis skirts and skorts on the golf course

A tennis skort works for golf when it meets the course's length rule. On petite golfers, a 13- or 14-inch tennis skort can land at mid-thigh and look just like a golf skort. On taller women, the same skort may sit higher than a strict club allows.

Two other differences to check:

  • Pockets: tennis skorts usually have ball pockets in the shorts, which work fine for a golf ball. Make sure there is room for tees and a marker.
  • Pleats and flounces: heavily flared tennis skirts can be a nuisance in wind on an open course. Flatter, A-line styles cross over best.

Tennis tops and tanks

Most tennis tanks lack a collar, so they are the piece least likely to pass a golf dress code. At relaxed public courses and ranges, a stylish tennis tank is often fine. At stricter clubs, swap it for a collared or mock-neck golf top.

Some tennis tops and dresses do have collars or zip mock necks; those cross over easily. Tennis tanks with a built-in bra are popular, but racerback cuts that show straps are the style most often listed as not allowed. See tennis tanks and golf tops.

Tennis dresses for golf

A tennis dress with a collar or mock neck and enough length usually works for golf. Collarless tennis dresses are fine at casual courses but may not pass at private clubs. Our golf dress guide covers what to look for if you want one dress for both sports.

Golf clothes on the tennis court

The crossover often works better the other way round. Golf skorts and golf dresses are generally fine for social tennis, as long as the fabric stretches enough for lunges. Some tennis clubs require predominantly white clothing, so check your club before wearing bright golf prints to a match.

Shoes are the one thing that never crosses over: golf shoes don't belong on a tennis court, and tennis shoes lack the grip and support for a golf swing on wet grass.

A quick check before you wear tennis clothes to a round

Run through four questions before you leave the house, and you will know whether your tennis outfit will work on the course.

  1. Does the top have a collar or mock neck? If not, is the course relaxed enough for collarless tops?
  2. Where does the skirt land when you bend? Crouch as if reading a putt. If you would feel more comfortable with more length, choose a golf skort instead.
  3. Is there a pocket for a ball, tee and marker? Four or five hours without pockets gets old quickly.
  4. Will it hold up for a long walk? Very lightweight tennis fabrics are great for an hour of play but can feel flimsy in wind or show sweat over a full round.

If the answer to any of these is "not sure", call the pro shop or pack a golf top as a backup.

How to build a two-sport wardrobe

If you play both, focus on pieces that suit golf's stricter rules, because they work for tennis too.

  • Skorts in the 15–17 inch range with ball pockets and stretch.
  • Sleeveless tops with a collar or zip mock neck.
  • A golf dress with built-in shorts.
  • Light layers: a quarter-zip and a vest.
  • Separate shoes for each sport.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear a tennis skirt to play golf?

Usually, if it is long enough for the course and you wear it with a collared or mock-neck top. Tennis skirts tend to be shorter than golf skorts, so taller women may find them too short for strict clubs. At public courses and ranges, a tennis skirt is almost always fine.

Are tennis tank tops allowed on golf courses?

At relaxed public courses, often yes. At many private clubs and resorts, collarless tanks aren't allowed, especially racerbacks and spaghetti straps. A sleeveless golf top with a collar or mock neck gives you the same cool feel while meeting almost every dress code.

What is the difference between a tennis skort and a golf skort?

Length and pockets, mostly. Tennis skorts are usually shorter, often around 13 to 14 inches, with ball pockets in the shorts. Golf skorts typically range from about 15 to 20 inches and often have extra pockets for tees and markers. Both have built-in shorts and stretch fabric.

Can I wear golf clothes to play tennis?

Generally yes. Golf skorts, dresses and sleeveless tops work well for social tennis if they stretch enough for quick movement. Check whether your tennis club requires white clothing, and always wear tennis shoes on the court, never golf shoes.

Lean toward golf's stricter rules when you buy, and most of your wardrobe will cover both sports with room to spare.
