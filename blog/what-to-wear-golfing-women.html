What to Wear Golfing: A Complete Outfit Guide for Women

By Fore Ladies Golf team · October 25, 2026

A safe women's golf outfit is a collared or mock-neck top, a skort, golf shorts, pants or a golf dress, and golf shoes. That combination passes the dress code at nearly every course, lets you swing freely and keeps you comfortable for four or five hours outside. Everything else — prints, colors, layers, accessories — is where your own style comes in.

Below we go piece by piece, then cover the dress-code questions women ask us most often, so you can walk onto any first tee without second-guessing what you put on that morning.

Start with the dress code, not the closet

The course decides what is acceptable, so check its rules before you plan an outfit. The Rules of Golf say nothing about clothing; every dress code you meet is set by the individual club or course.

Most courses fall into one of three groups:

  • Private clubs and resorts: usually the strictest. Expect collars or mock necks, no denim, a minimum length for skirts, skorts and shorts, and golf shoes only.
  • Daily-fee and semi-private courses: "golf attire" with a bit more flexibility — sleeveless tops with a collar are usually fine, and some allow crew necks.
  • Municipal courses, par-3s and driving ranges: often relaxed. Clean athletic clothes and sneakers are common.

If the website is vague, call the pro shop and ask two questions: "Are sleeveless tops okay?" and "Is there a length rule for skorts?" Those two answers settle most outfits.

Tops: collars, mock necks and sleeveless styles

A collared polo shirt is the most widely accepted golf top for women, and a mock-neck top is a close second. Both look tidy and protect the back of your neck from the sun.

Sleeveless golf shirts are common in women's golf and allowed at most courses as long as they have a collar or mock neck. A sleeveless top without a collar — a racerback tank, for example — is the piece most likely to be questioned at a stricter club.

Look for performance fabric: a polyester and spandex blend that dries quickly and stretches as you rotate. Cotton feels nice at breakfast and heavy by the back nine. Browse our women's golf tops, or narrow it down to sleeveless, short sleeve and long sleeve styles.

Bottoms: skorts, shorts, pants and capris

The golf skort is the most popular bottom for women because it looks like a skirt and works like shorts. Built-in shorts mean you can bend to tee up, read a putt or climb into a cart without thinking about it.

How long should it be? Many clubs that set a rule ask for something close to mid-thigh, and skorts are sold by length, usually measured from waistband to hem. We explain the numbers in our golf skort length guide.

Other good options:

  • Golf shorts: tailored, with belt loops and a modest inseam. Running shorts and bike shorts usually aren't accepted at stricter courses.
  • Golf pants and ankle pants: the most covered and dress-code-proof choice, great for cool mornings. If you are under about 5'4", our petite fit guide explains inseams.
  • Capris: accepted at most courses, comfortable on warm days when you still want coverage.

Pockets matter more than people expect. You will want somewhere for a ball marker, a tee and a spare ball. Shop golf skorts, golf shorts and golf pants.

Golf dresses: one piece, done

A golf dress is a complete outfit in one piece, and nearly every golf dress has a collar or mock neck so it fits dress codes. Most also include separate shorts or a built-in short, which is what separates a golf dress from a summer dress.

Fit is the thing to get right: the waist should sit at your natural waist so the dress moves with your swing instead of riding up. Our golf dress buying guide covers torso length, shorts and pockets, and you can browse styles in golf dresses.

Shoes and socks

Wear golf shoes on the course. Spikeless golf shoes look like sneakers and grip well in dry conditions; shoes with soft spikes grip better on wet grass and hills. Many courses don't allow metal spikes, and some stricter clubs don't allow regular sneakers on the course.

Pair them with no-show or low socks in a moisture-wicking fabric. Our guide to choosing women's golf shoes walks through waterproofing, width and comfort for 18 holes. See also women's golf shoes.

Layers, hats and the small things

Bring one more layer than you think you need. Mornings on a golf course are cooler than the forecast suggests, and it is easier to take a layer off on the fourth hole than to wish you had one on the first.

  • Layers: a lightweight quarter-zip pullover, a vest or a wind jacket. Vests keep your core warm without restricting your arms. See golf outerwear.
  • Hat or visor: sun protection for your face and scalp. Visors are cooler; caps cover more.
  • Golf glove: most right-handed golfers wear one on the left hand for grip.
  • Belt: expected at stricter clubs when your bottoms have belt loops.

Frequently asked questions

Can women wear leggings to play golf?

Many public courses allow golf leggings, especially in cooler weather, but stricter clubs often don't allow them worn on their own. A safe compromise is leggings under a skort or a golf dress. When in doubt, call the pro shop, because rules on leggings vary more than almost any other item.

Do women have to wear a collared shirt for golf?

Not everywhere, but a collar or mock neck is the safest choice. Many courses accept collarless women's golf tops, and sleeveless styles are widely accepted when they have a collar. Private clubs are the most likely to insist on a collar, so check before you visit one for the first time.

Can I wear jeans to a golf course?

Usually not on the course itself. Denim is one of the most common items banned in golf dress codes, including at many relaxed public courses. Jeans are often fine at the driving range or in the clubhouse restaurant, but for a round, choose golf pants, capris, shorts or a skort instead.

What should I wear golfing for the first time?

Keep it simple: a collared or mock-neck top, golf shorts or a skort, comfortable athletic shoes or golf shoes, a hat and sunscreen. If you are only going to the range or a beginner lesson, clean athletic clothes are usually fine. Our first lesson outfit guide goes into more detail.

How do I know my size in golf clothes?

Measure your bust, natural waist, hips and inseam once with a soft tape, then compare those numbers with each brand's size chart instead of relying on the size you usually buy. Golf brands size differently from each other. Our size charts and fit guide explains how to measure.

The short version: pick a collared top, a bottom with built-in shorts or a tailored fit, and golf shoes, then check the course rules. After that, wear what makes you feel like yourself — that confidence shows up in your swing, too.
