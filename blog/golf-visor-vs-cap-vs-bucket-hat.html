Golf Visors vs Caps vs Bucket Hats: Which Should You Wear?

By Fore Ladies Golf team · November 21, 2026

If sun protection is your top priority, a bucket hat or wide-brim hat beats a cap, and a cap beats a visor. A visor is the coolest and easiest with a ponytail, but it leaves the top of your head, your ears and the back of your neck in the sun. A cap shades your eyes and scalp and works almost everywhere, including strict clubs. A bucket hat with a brim of two to three inches all the way around covers the most skin, which makes it the smart pick for long, bright rounds.

Which golf hat gives the best sun protection?

A hat with a brim that goes all the way around protects the most. The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends a three-inch brim all around, tightly woven material, and fabric rated UPF 50 or higher. MD Anderson Cancer Center gives similar advice: a brim at least two to three inches wide that shades your face, ears and neck.

That same MD Anderson article is blunt about the other two styles. It says visors leave the top of your head, your ears and the back of your neck exposed, and that baseball caps only protect part of your face. Cancer Council NSW in Australia says caps and visors don't protect the cheeks, ears and back of the neck.

No hat covers everything, though. The Skin Cancer Foundation points to a study in which none of the hats tested protected the whole face and neck from every direction. Sunscreen and sunglasses still belong in your bag. When is a golf visor the right choice?

A visor is best when you want shade for your eyes and the least heat on your head. With no crown, it lets heat escape from the top of your head, which many golfers like on humid days. It also leaves room for any hairstyle: high ponytail, bun, braids or curls.

Visors make the most sense for early tee times, cloudy days and short practice sessions. Slide-on and coil visors come off without flattening your hair.

The trade-off: your part, crown, ears and neck stay in the sun all round. If you love a visor, put sunscreen on your part line and ears before you tee off, and reapply at the turn.

When is a golf cap the right choice?

A cap is the all-rounder. It shades your eyes and covers your scalp, it fits under an umbrella or a rain hood, and it stays on in a breeze. Worn facing forward, it fits almost any dress code.

Look for a cap with an adjustable back strap or a ponytail opening so it fits with your hair up. Lightweight polyester caps with an inner sweatband dry faster than heavy cotton, and lighter colors feel cooler in direct sun (MD Anderson notes that lighter colors reflect more of the sun's rays).

The weak spots are your ears, cheeks and neck. On a long summer round, pair a cap with sunscreen on those spots, a collared shirt to cover the back of your neck, or a long-sleeve golf top with UPF protection.

When is a bucket hat or wide-brim hat the right choice?

Choose a bucket hat or wide-brim hat when you'll be in strong sun for hours, such as a summer 18 holes or a sunny golf trip. A brim that circles your head shades your ears and neck as well as your face.

For real protection, look for these details:

  • Brim width: two to three inches or more, all the way around.
  • Tight weave: MD Anderson warns that straw hats often have large gaps in the weave. Hold the hat up to the light; if you can see light through it, so can the sun.
  • UPF rating: UPF 50+ is the strongest rating; UPF 30 to 49 is still good protection.
  • Chin cord or snug fit: wide brims catch the wind. A cord keeps your hat from bouncing down the fairway.
  • Brim stiffness: a soft brim that flops into your eyes is a distraction over the ball. A slightly stiffened brim or a shorter front brim makes it easier to see your target.

If a wide brim gets in the way when you look down at the ball, try a medium brim instead.

Are bucket hats and visors allowed by golf dress codes?

Visors and caps are accepted at nearly every course. Bucket hats are common at public courses, but some traditional private clubs prefer classic golf caps and visors. The Rules of Golf don't regulate what you wear, so headwear rules come from each club.

The most common hat rules are about how and where you wear them. Firestone Country Club's dress code says baseball-style hats may not be worn backwards and that hats aren't permitted at dinner in its grille. Aliso Viejo Country Club's dress code requires hats to face forward on the course. Golf.com's etiquette column suggests that at a private club, you take your hat off inside the clubhouse.

So the safe plan: wear your hat facing forward, take it off in the dining room, and check your club's written rules if you're a guest. Our women's golf dress code guide covers the rest of what clubs usually expect.

How to choose the right golf hat for you

Match the hat to how long and bright your rounds are:

  • Short rounds, cool or cloudy days, hair up: a visor.
  • Everyday rounds, breezy days, strict clubs: a cap.
  • Long rounds in strong sun, sunny travel destinations: a bucket or wide-brim hat with a tight weave.

Fit matters too: adjustable straps and inner sweatbands make the biggest difference. If you're heading somewhere sunny this winter, our guide to what to wear golfing in Florida in winter and our golf trip packing list show where a hat fits into the rest of your bag.

Frequently asked questions

Is a visor or a cap better for golf?

A cap gives better sun protection because it covers the top of your head, while a visor leaves your scalp, ears and neck exposed. A visor is cooler and easier with a high ponytail or bun. If you prefer a visor, put sunscreen on your part line, ears and neck before every round.

Can you wear a bucket hat golfing?

Yes, at most public courses a bucket hat is perfectly fine, and it protects more skin than a cap or visor. Some traditional private clubs prefer classic caps and visors, so check the club's dress code if you're a guest. Choose one with a chin cord or snug fit for windy days.

How wide should a golf sun hat's brim be?

The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends a brim of about three inches all the way around, and MD Anderson Cancer Center suggests at least two to three inches. A brim that circles your head shades your ears and the back of your neck, which caps and visors don't cover.

Do you have to take your hat off in the clubhouse?

It depends on the club. Many private clubs expect hats off indoors or in the dining room, and some ban backwards caps everywhere. Public courses are usually more relaxed. If you're not sure, read the posted dress code or take your hat off when you sit down to eat.

Do I still need sunscreen if I wear a golf hat?

Yes. No hat protects your whole face and neck from every angle, and sunlight reflects up from sand, water and pale surfaces. Use sunscreen on any skin your hat doesn't shade, wear sunglasses, and reapply sunscreen during the round, especially on long, bright days.
