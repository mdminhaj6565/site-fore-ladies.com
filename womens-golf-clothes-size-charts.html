Size Charts

How to measure yourself properly — once

Almost every sizing problem in online clothes shopping traces back to a guess. Spend ten minutes with a soft tape measure and a note on your phone, and you will buy more accurately for years. Measure over the underwear you would normally wear, keep the tape snug but not tight, and keep it level all the way round.

Bust: around the fullest part, with the tape level across your back. Natural waist: the narrowest point of your torso, usually just above the navel — bend sideways and the crease that forms is your natural waist. Hip: around the fullest part of your hips and seat, usually about 20cm below the waist. Inseam: from the crotch seam to where you want the hem to fall, measured on a pair of pants you already like rather than on your leg. Torso length: from the top of your shoulder, over the bust, down to your natural waist — this is the measurement that decides whether a dress sits where it should.

Write all five down. When a size chart gives you two plausible options, these numbers settle it.

Why golf brands size so differently

There is no shared standard. A size 8 from a Scandinavian brand, an American brand and an Italian brand can differ by several centimetres in the same measurement, and the differences are not consistent across the body — a brand that runs generous in the hip may run narrow in the shoulder.

Three factors drive most of the variation. First, the base block each brand designs from, which reflects the body shape it has historically fitted. Second, the intended fit — performance brands cut closer, traditional brands cut looser, and both may label the result the same size. Third, fabric stretch: a garment with four-way stretch can be cut smaller than a woven one and still fit, so the flat measurement alone does not tell the whole story.

The practical conclusion is simple. Trust the brand's own chart and your measurements, not the size you wore last time in something else. It is entirely normal to be three different sizes across three brands in your wardrobe, and it says nothing about you.

Choosing skort and skirt length

Golf skorts and skirts are listed by the length of the skirt portion, measured from the waistband down. Common lengths run from about 15 inches to about 20 inches, and the right number depends on your height and on where you want the hem to sit relative to your knee.

The reliable method is to measure a skort you already own and wear happily, then shop to that number. If you do not own one, measure from your natural waist to the point on your leg where you would like the hem, and use that. Height matters more than dress size here: the same 18-inch skort sits well above the knee on a 5'2" frame and close to it on a 5'9" one.

Many clubs and courses have dress codes that specify a minimum length, so if you play somewhere with published rules, check them before ordering. Our petite collection and plus size collection both address the cases where standard lengths do not translate well.

Pants, shorts and the inseam question

Golf pants are cut deliberately long, because they are meant to break over the top of a golf shoe rather than sit above it. This surprises people who buy their usual size and find several extra inches at the hem. It is intentional, not an error — but it does mean that inseam matters at least as much as waist size.

Check the inseam before you check the size. Many brands offer the same pant in multiple inseams, listed in the product name as a number such as 29 or 32. Shorts and cropped pants are listed the same way. If your inseam falls between two options, remember that hemming is a straightforward and inexpensive alteration while lengthening is not.

Tops, shoulders and the swing test

For tops, the shoulder is the measurement that decides whether a garment works for golf. A shoulder seam should sit at the outer edge of your shoulder bone. If it has slipped down your arm, the top is too large through the upper body regardless of how it fits at the waist, and it will bind when you rotate.

When a top arrives, do the swing test before you remove the tags. Stand as you would at address, make a slow, full backswing and follow-through, then reach across your body. If the fabric pulls across the shoulder blades, rides up at the front, or the cuff drags over the back of your hand, it is the wrong size or the wrong cut. A top that passes that test will be comfortable for eighteen holes.

Sleeveless styles remove the sleeve variable entirely and are the most forgiving choice if you are uncertain, which is one reason they dominate summer golf wardrobes.

Dresses and one-piece fit

Golf and tennis dresses are the hardest garment to size online, because they have to fit the bust, the waist and the hip simultaneously, and the torso length has to be right as well. If a brand's chart gives a dress length or a torso measurement, use it — it tells you more than the size does.

Styles with a defined or adjustable waist, a wrap front, or an elasticated seam are considerably more forgiving than a straight shift. If you are between sizes in a dress, the larger size is usually the safer choice, since a dress that is slightly loose can be belted while one that is tight across the hip cannot be fixed.

Shoes: width matters as much as length

Golf shoe sizing varies between European and American systems, and conversions are not always consistent between brands. Measure your foot at the end of the day when it is at its largest, and measure both feet — most people have one slightly larger, and the shoe should fit that one.

Width is the measurement people most often ignore and most often need. If golf shoes have consistently felt tight across the ball of your foot, the answer is a wider fit rather than a longer shoe. Going up a size to solve a width problem gives you a shoe that slides at the heel, which costs you stability in the swing. There is more on this in our golf shoes collection.

What to do when you are between sizes

There is no universal rule, but there are useful defaults. For bottoms, size to your largest relevant measurement — usually hip — and alter the waist if needed. For tops, size to the shoulder. For dresses, size up. For anything with significant stretch, size down. For anything marked final sale, ask us first rather than guessing.

Our returns policy covers new, unworn items within 30 days of delivery, which means that on regular-price items, ordering the size you believe is right and returning it if it is not is a perfectly reasonable approach.

Still not sure? Ask us

Charts cannot answer everything. If you want a flat garment measurement, the fabric composition, or an honest opinion on how a particular style runs, get in touch through our contact page. We will measure the actual piece rather than quote the chart back at you. Finding the right size the first time is better for you and better for us.
